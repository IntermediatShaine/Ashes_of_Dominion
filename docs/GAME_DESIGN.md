# ASHES OF DOMINION

## Comprehensive Game Design Document

**Genre:** Medieval Fantasy / Action RPG / Settlement Management / Strategy / Exploration
**Art Style:** 2.5D Pixel Art
**Platform:** Windows PC
**Controls:** Mouse and Keyboard
**Game Mode:** Single-player
**Development:** Claude-assisted development using Godot and GDScript
**Current Objective:** Develop a functional, expandable prototype before progressing toward the complete game.

---

# 1. MY OVERALL VISION

I am developing a medieval fantasy pixel-art game called **Ashes of Dominion**.

My vision is to create a game that combines several different genres into one interconnected experience.

I want the game to incorporate:

* Real-time action RPG combat.
* Character development and evolving classes.
* Hero and villain progression.
* Exploration and procedural world generation.
* Strategic movement across a large world map.
* Settlement construction and management.
* Population management and recruitment.
* Army creation and military strategy.
* Dynamic factions, alliances, and territorial warfare.
* Meaningful choices with lasting consequences.
* A changing world that reacts to the player's decisions.
* High replayability through different starting characters, world settings, and randomized exploration.

The game should not force me into becoming either a hero or a villain.

Instead, I want the world to respond naturally to my actions.

I should be able to begin as a morally good character and gradually descend into tyranny, or begin as a ruthless individual and eventually redeem myself.

These changes should influence far more than dialogue.

They should affect the way I fight, the people who follow me, how I govern settlements, how civilizations perceive me, and what abilities or classes become available.

I want the experience to evolve from being a lone survivor struggling to stay alive into potentially becoming a powerful adventurer, ruler, conqueror, or even a legendary figure capable of changing the world.

However, reaching such a position should require considerable effort.

I do not want powerful equipment, companions, armies, or settlements to be handed to me immediately.

**Progression, discovery, survival, and consequences should form the foundation of the game.**

# 2. THE WORLD AND ITS SETTING

## 2.1 A World Combining Multiple Fantasy Genres

Although I want Ashes of Dominion to fundamentally be a medieval fantasy game, I do not want it restricted to a single interpretation of medieval fantasy.

Instead, I want the world divided into different regions, civilizations, and environments, each representing distinct fantasy themes.

The four primary themes are:

### Dark Medieval Fantasy

This represents the more grounded and brutal aspects of medieval civilization.

Examples include:

* Human kingdoms.
* Feudal societies.
* Castles and fortified towns.
* Territorial disputes.
* Religious conflicts.
* Mercenary companies.
* Corrupt rulers.
* Bandits and raiders.
* Poverty and political instability.

The environment should feel dangerous, with survival depending on preparation and resources.

### High Fantasy

These regions contain more traditional magical civilizations.

Examples include:

* Elven kingdoms.
* Enchanted forests.
* Magical academies.
* Ancient dragons.
* Magical creatures.
* Powerful artifacts.
* Legendary heroes.
* Supernatural environments.

These areas can be more visually colorful and fantastical than the darker medieval regions.

However, their inhabitants should still participate in political conflicts, wars, and territorial disputes.

### Post-Apocalyptic Fantasy

Parts of the world should show evidence of civilizations that have already collapsed.

Examples include:

* Destroyed cities.
* Abandoned fortresses.
* Wastelands.
* Corrupted forests.
* Former battlefields.
* Magical disasters.
* Collapsed empires.
* Areas overrun by monsters.
* Scattered survivors living among ancient ruins.

These regions should provide particularly dangerous exploration opportunities.

They may also contain valuable resources, forgotten knowledge, and abandoned technology.

### Dark Fantasy with Ancient Technology

I want certain civilizations or locations to contain remnants of technology that no longer belong to the current historical period.

This technology does not need to resemble conventional modern technology.

It can instead involve:

* Ancient magical machinery.
* Forgotten mechanical constructs.
* Arcane engines.
* Autonomous guardians.
* Lost underground facilities.
* Mechanically enhanced creatures.
* Machinery powered by supernatural forces.
* Artifacts combining engineering and magic.

I want this technology to feel mysterious and potentially dangerous.

It should suggest that the world has a much deeper history than what its present inhabitants understand.

## 2.2 A Unified World

These four themes should not exist as completely separate games.

Instead, they should coexist within the same world.

Different regions can have radically different cultures, technology, magic, architecture, geography, and political structures.

Civilizations should influence one another through trade, diplomacy, conquest, migration, and conflict.

For example, a human kingdom might form an alliance with a dwarven civilization to resist an expanding demonic faction.

Elsewhere, an elven kingdom could become involved in a territorial conflict with humans.

A civilization controlling ancient technology might become a major military threat despite having a relatively small population.

These relationships should evolve dynamically throughout a playthrough.

The player should be able to influence these relationships.

# 4. THE BEGINNING OF THE STORY

## 4.1 My Starting Village

The game begins in a small village located within a contested region of the world.

The village is situated in what is effectively **no-man's-land**, surrounded by competing factions and civilizations.

None of these factions has complete control over the territory.

Consequently, the area frequently experiences:

* Territorial disputes.
* Military incursions.
* Raiding parties.
* Conflicts between neighboring civilizations.
* Political instability.
* Refugee movements.
* Resource shortages.

Eventually, the conflict reaches my village.

The village is destroyed, or catastrophically devastated, during one of these conflicts.

I survive the destruction.

My character becomes the central protagonist of the story.

## 4.2 The Chosen One

My character is not simply another ordinary survivor.

There is something fundamentally different about them.

They are effectively a chosen one, although I do not necessarily want their true significance revealed immediately.

This distinction becomes particularly important through the game's death and resurrection mechanics.

However, being chosen should not mean starting with overwhelming power.

I want to begin weak, relatively inexperienced, and without a large support network.

I should have to build my strength through exploration, fighting, training, relationships, and difficult decisions.

## 4.3 My Long-Term Objectives

The main narrative should develop through multiple interconnected story arcs.

The initial objective revolves around surviving and understanding the destruction of my home.

Over time, I want the story to expand into:

**Revenge:** Discovering who was responsible for the destruction of my village and deciding what to do about it.

**Conspiracy:** Discovering that the conflict may involve more than ordinary territorial disputes.

**Dominion:** Eventually gaining enough influence to change the balance of power within the world.

My ultimate choices could involve:

* Saving threatened civilizations.
* Rebuilding destroyed communities.
* Establishing my own kingdom.
* Uniting opposing factions.
* Taking control of existing kingdoms.
* Becoming a feared conqueror.
* Destroying civilizations.
* Pursuing personal power regardless of the consequences.

I do not want one predetermined ending.

The story should respond to the character I become.

# 5. CHARACTER CREATION AND PLAYABLE RACES

## 5.1 Multiple Starting Characters

I want the player to have several possible starting characters rather than one completely fixed protagonist.

Character creation should meaningfully affect gameplay.

Different races and backgrounds should have different relationships with existing civilizations.

This means my starting reputation should depend partly on the character I choose.

A character belonging to one civilization might receive favorable treatment from their own people while being distrusted by their enemies.

Another race could experience entirely different political and social relationships.

## 5.2 Proposed Playable Races

The current proposed selection includes:

1. Humans.
2. Elves.
3. Dwarves.
4. Orcs.
5. Draconic races.
6. Demonkin.
7. Celestial races.
8. Mixed-blood characters.

These are proposed starting options, not necessarily the complete or finalized roster.

I want the different races to influence both roleplaying and gameplay.

Potential differences include physical attributes, magical affinities, relationships with factions, and social restrictions.

The exact racial bonuses and penalties still need to be balanced.

## 5.3 Mixed-Blood Characters

One feature I specifically want is the ability to combine two races.

Examples might include:

* Elf and Demonkin.
* Dwarf and Orc.
* Celestial and Demonkin.
* Other combinations that make sense within the world's lore.

However, mixed ancestry should have a substantial disadvantage.

**Because the character is mixed-blood, they should begin with extremely poor standing among nearly every civilization.**

This represents a character who struggles to find acceptance anywhere.

Such a character might experience:

* Hostility from multiple civilizations.
* Difficulty entering settlements.
* Reduced access to certain services.
* Discrimination from NPCs.
* Restrictions on recruitment.
* Problems establishing diplomatic relationships.
* Increased difficulty earning trust.

This should make playing as a mixed-blood character considerably more challenging.

However, I do not want the disadvantage to make the character permanently unplayable.

A sufficiently determined player should be able to overcome some of these barriers through exceptional actions, influence, military strength, or reputation.

Mixed-blood characters could potentially have unusual combinations of abilities as compensation, although the exact mechanics are not finalized.

# 6. HERO AND VILLAIN SYSTEM

This is one of the most important systems I want in the game.

## 6.1 Dynamic Morality

I do not want characters permanently assigned to either the hero or villain category.

Instead, morality should evolve throughout the game.

My actions should influence how the world perceives me.

For example:

A heroic character who begins conquering villages, executing prisoners, and enslaving populations should gradually become feared and hated.

Likewise, a former villain who begins rescuing prisoners, protecting settlements, and rebuilding communities should have opportunities for redemption.

I want these transitions to have serious consequences.

They should not simply change a single morality number.

## 6.2 Morality and Reputation Are Different Systems

I want personal morality and faction reputation to be tracked separately.

This distinction is important.

For example, I might be considered a hero by one kingdom because I protected its citizens.

However, a neighboring kingdom might consider me an enemy because I fought against its soldiers.

Similarly, a morally corrupt character could maintain excellent diplomatic relations with another corrupt civilization.

This means a character should be able to have:

* High morality and poor faction reputation.
* Low morality and excellent faction reputation.
* High morality and excellent faction reputation.
* Low morality and poor faction reputation.

These relationships should also change independently.

## 6.3 Consequences of Morality

My moral alignment should influence:

* Character abilities.
* Class development.
* NPC interactions.
* Companion loyalty.
* Recruitment opportunities.
* Settlement governance.
* Diplomatic relationships.
* Population behavior.
* Available quests.
* Story progression.
* How enemies react to me.

The more significant my actions become, the more significant the consequences should be.

I want morality to influence the entire experience rather than functioning as a cosmetic alignment system.

# 7. CHARACTER CLASSES AND PROGRESSION

## 7.1 Starting Class: Survivor

I want the player to begin with a relatively generic class called **Survivor**.

At the beginning of the game, the protagonist should not already be a powerful knight, necromancer, or legendary warrior.

Instead, the character develops through gameplay.

This allows progression to feel earned.

## 7.2 Specialization and Class Evolution

I want a combination of two progression systems.

First, my character should specialize according to the skills and activities I pursue.

Second, these specializations should eventually evolve into more powerful classes.

For example:

**Survivor → Combat Specialization → Advanced Class → Heroic or Villainous Evolution**

The exact number of progression tiers is not yet finalized.

The important requirement is that the character's progression reflects their behavior, abilities, and moral development.

I do not want leveling to consist exclusively of increasing numerical statistics.

New classes should introduce meaningful changes to gameplay.

## 7.3 Proposed Advanced Classes

We previously outlined ten potential class families with heroic and villainous specializations.

| Heroic Specialization | Villainous Specialization |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Paladin               | Overlord                  |
| Arcanist              | Voidcaller                |
| Ranger                | Shadowblade               |
| Lifewarden            | Necromancer               |
| Vanguard Engineer     | Machinist                 |
| Ascendant Monk        | Plaguebearer              |
| Dragon Knight         | Bloodlord                 |
| Sentinel              | Harbinger                 |
| Chronokeeper          | Puppeteer                 |
| Celestial Guardian    | Fallen Seraph             |

This provides a potential starting framework of **20 advanced specializations**.

However, these names and pairings are still proposals rather than a finalized class system.

I want them refined according to balance, world lore, and gameplay.

## 7.4 Different Progression Methods

I want certain classes to grow stronger through activities that fit their identity.

For example, heroic classes might progress through:

* Protecting settlements.
* Saving civilians.
* Building alliances.
* Fulfilling important oaths.
* Defeating dangerous enemies.
* Establishing positive reputations.

Villainous classes might progress through:

* Conquering territory.
* Spreading fear.
* Commanding armies.
* Performing forbidden research.
* Acquiring forbidden knowledge.
* Corrupting individuals or locations.
* Expanding their influence.

This should not mean that the player is forced into one repetitive activity.

Instead, progression should reward behavior consistent with the character's developing identity.

## 7.5 Changing Alignment

A character who changes their morality should not necessarily retain every benefit of their previous alignment without consequences.

I want class development and morality to interact.

For example, a corrupted heroic character might lose access to certain powers while developing darker abilities.

A villain pursuing redemption might gradually gain access to different forms of progression.

The precise mechanics of class conversion, retained abilities, and penalties still require development.

# 8. REAL-TIME COMBAT

## 8.1 Combat Philosophy

I want combat to take place in real time.

The player should control their character directly during encounters.

Combat should feel responsive and should support different fighting styles depending on the selected class.

The system should eventually accommodate:

* Melee combat.
* Ranged combat.
* Magic.
* Defensive abilities.
* Movement-based combat.
* Special class abilities.
* Companion assistance.
* Encounters involving multiple enemies.

The exact combat mechanics and ability systems remain to be expanded.

## 8.2 Player and Army Combat

I want to distinguish between two different types of combat.

**Personal combat** occurs when I directly control my character during exploration and smaller encounters.

**Military combat** occurs when I command larger groups of soldiers.

I do not want to manually control every individual soldier during battles.

Instead, soldiers should be capable of fighting automatically according to commands and strategic objectives.

This allows me to participate in combat while simultaneously influencing the larger conflict.

The military system should emphasize command and strategy rather than individual unit micromanagement.

---

# 9. COMPANIONS AND PARTY SYSTEM

## 9.1 Starting Alone

I want the game to begin with my protagonist alone.

Companions should not be freely available immediately.

Recruiting important companions should require effort, exploration, or unusual circumstances.

## 9.2 Main Companions

I want the player to be able to acquire up to **three important companions**.

These should be relatively rare characters rather than disposable units.

Each should feel significant.

I want them to have individual identities, relationships, and potential story consequences.

The exact companion progression and equipment systems are still open for expansion.

## 9.3 Companion Mortality

Unlike the protagonist, companions should not automatically return after dying.

I want companion death to be permanent.

This makes decisions involving dangerous encounters more meaningful.

I should have to consider whether a particular expedition is worth risking an important companion.

## 9.4 Captured Companions

Companions should also be capable of being captured by enemy factions.

Capture should not always result in immediate death.

Instead, enemies might demand an expensive ransom.

This creates additional strategic decisions.

I might have to choose between paying the ransom, abandoning the companion, or attempting to recover them through other gameplay systems.

The exact recovery methods remain to be designed.

## 9.5 Companions Versus Armies

I want a clear distinction between main companions and ordinary soldiers.

**Main companions** are rare, individually important characters who accompany me on adventures.

**Armies** consist of larger groups of recruited units who participate in military operations.

The three-companion limit should not mean I can only command three soldiers.

It applies to the important companion system rather than the full military system.

---

# 10. PLAYER DEATH AND RESURRECTION

## 10.1 The Protagonist Cannot Permanently Die

My character is unique because of the chosen-one aspect of the story.

When my character dies, they should return to the village through a supernatural curse or blessing.

This return should be tied to the game's lore.

I do not want resurrection to feel like an unrelated gameplay convenience.

## 10.2 Death Should Still Matter

Although my character can return, death should not necessarily be free.

I want the resurrection mechanic to create consequences.

The precise punishment system remains undecided, but the general intention is that dying should still be meaningful.

Potential consequences could eventually involve lost resources, temporary weakening, or other effects consistent with the curse or blessing.

These are possibilities rather than confirmed mechanics.

## 10.3 The Difference Between Player and Companion Death

This distinction is fundamental.

**The protagonist returns after death. Companions do not.**

This should influence how I approach difficult encounters.

I might be willing to risk my own character's life, but exposing an important companion to the same danger could permanently alter my playthrough.

---

# 11. EXPLORATION AND WORLD NAVIGATION

Exploration is another major part of my vision.

I want the navigation system to combine strategic planning with actual physical exploration.

## 11.1 Two Exploration Perspectives

I want two complementary views.

### Strategic World Map

The world map should allow me to see known settlements, regions, landmarks, and routes.

I should be able to select destinations and plan expeditions.

This is where broader strategic decisions take place.

### Three-Quarter Exploration View

When physically exploring an area, I want a three-quarter, top-down perspective.

This should provide a more immersive environment in which I directly control my character.

The perspective should work well with the game's pixel-art presentation.

I do not want the entire game restricted to a simple overhead strategy map.

---

# 12. THE SHIFTING WORLD AND PROCEDURAL GENERATION

This is one of the more unusual features I want.

## 12.1 Randomized but Familiar

I want the world to feel both randomized and recognizable.

Whenever I restart the world, certain parts of the environment should change.

However, important locations should remain consistent.

For example:

* Major villages remain in their established locations.
* Important cities remain recognizable.
* Story-related landmarks remain in place.
* Specific NPC locations can remain consistent where required by the story.
* Important permanent structures maintain their narrative significance.

Meanwhile, the terrain and routes connecting these locations can change.

## 12.2 Procedural Wilderness

The wilderness should be generated procedurally.

Potential changes include:

* Forest layouts.
* Roads and trails.
* Obstacles.
* Traversable routes.
* Resource locations.
* Encounter locations.
* Environmental hazards.

This gives the game replayability while preserving its overall narrative structure.

## 12.3 Persistent Landmarks

The system should distinguish between two layers.

**Persistent world layer:** Important settlements, major landmarks, and narrative locations.

**Procedural world layer:** Wilderness, smaller routes, resources, environmental obstacles, and other variable content.

I want these layers to work together rather than compete with one another.

The player should be able to remember important locations from previous experiences without knowing exactly how to reach them every time.

## 12.4 World Seeds

The world-generation system should support deterministic procedural generation through seeds.

This means the same seed and world-generation rules can reproduce the same procedural geography.

However, a new world can generate different terrain while maintaining the established locations required by the game's narrative.

This is particularly important for saving, loading, and troubleshooting the game.

---

# 13. THE MIST OF DISCOVERY

I want a special exploration mechanic that makes traveling through the world more uncertain.

We have referred to this as the **Mist of Discovery**.

## 13.1 Basic Function

Unknown parts of the world should be concealed by a form of fog of war.

The player must explore these areas to discover their contents.

This should affect both the map and strategic decisions.

## 13.2 World Restarts

When beginning a new world, much of the terrain should become unknown again.

Even if I remember where an important city is located, I should not automatically know the precise route required to reach it.

Because procedural geography may change, exploration becomes necessary again.

This is how I want to combine familiarity with uncertainty.

## 13.3 Optional Fog Settings

The Mist of Discovery should be configurable.

I want players to be able to enable or disable it through world settings.

Some players may prefer a more conventional exploration experience.

Others may want the full difficulty of navigating an uncertain world.

The system should support both.

---

# 14. PROCEDURAL ROUTE PLANNING

I want route planning to be an actual gameplay mechanic.

## 14.1 Multiple Routes

When selecting a destination, I want the game to calculate possible routes.

These routes should account for factors such as:

* Terrain.
* Distance.
* Environmental danger.
* Available paths.
* Known obstacles.
* Resource requirements.
* Potential enemy encounters.

The player should not necessarily be forced to accept the first route suggested.

I want the ability to choose between routes and potentially modify the planned journey.

## 14.2 Estimated Information

I specifically do not want the game to reveal perfectly accurate information about every route.

Instead, route previews should provide estimates.

For example, a route could indicate:

* Relatively low danger.
* Moderate resource requirements.
* A potentially longer journey.
* Uncertain enemy activity.
* Possible environmental hazards.

I want players to make decisions using imperfect information.

This creates tension and rewards preparation.

## 14.3 Changing Routes

Because the surrounding wilderness can be procedurally generated, the best route between two important locations might change between worlds.

For example, a route that was previously safe could become considerably more dangerous in a new playthrough.

This should encourage players to explore and reassess their plans.

## 14.4 Permanent Magical Shortcuts

I also want a rare mechanic involving magical shortcuts.

Certain unusual NPCs or events might reward the player with magical scrolls.

These scrolls could reveal or establish permanent shortcuts between locations.

Importantly, these shortcuts should remain available regardless of the normal Mist of Discovery mechanics.

Such rewards should be rare.

I want them to feel valuable because they provide a significant strategic advantage.

# 15. FACTIONS AND POLITICAL RELATIONSHIPS

## 15.1 Independent Civilizations

The world should contain multiple competing civilizations.

Potential faction types include:

* Human kingdoms.
* Elven kingdoms.
* Dwarven civilizations.
* Orcish factions.
* Demonic warlords.
* Necromantic factions.
* Civilizations associated with ancient technology.

These are proposed faction categories rather than a finalized faction roster.

## 15.2 Dynamic Diplomacy

I want factions to interact with each other independently of the player.

They should be capable of:

* Forming alliances.
* Declaring wars.
* Establishing trade relationships.
* Negotiating peace.
* Conquering territory.
* Losing territory.
* Expanding their influence.
* Developing rivalries.
* Responding to emerging threats.

I do not want the world to remain static while I explore.

A conflict should be capable of progressing even when I am not directly involved.

## 15.3 Territorial Warfare

Territory should matter.

Different factions should attempt to control valuable locations.

This includes settlements, resources, strategic routes, and important geographical positions.

The contested region surrounding my starting village should be particularly unstable.

## 15.4 Player Influence

My actions should be capable of changing political relationships.

For example, helping one faction defeat another could dramatically improve my standing with the victor while damaging relations with its enemies.

I should eventually be able to influence conflicts through diplomacy, military strength, or other actions.

The world should remember significant events.

---

# 16. ENEMY AWARENESS AND THE FIVE-EXPEDITION GRACE PERIOD

I want enemy factions to become aware of the player's growing settlement and influence.

However, I do not want overwhelming enemy forces attacking immediately.

## 16.1 Initial Grace Period

I want approximately **five exploration expeditions** before enemy discovery becomes a serious threat.

This gives the player time to:

* Learn the basic mechanics.
* Collect resources.
* Explore the surrounding area.
* Establish essential infrastructure.
* Prepare defenses.

The exact interaction between this grace period and different difficulty settings may require balancing.

## 16.2 Increasing Awareness

After the initial grace period, nearby enemy factions should gradually become more aware of the player.

Their response should depend on the circumstances.

Potential factors include:

* The player's military strength.
* Settlement development.
* Political relationships.
* Territorial expansion.
* Reputation.
* Difficulty settings.
* Previous encounters.

I want increasing danger to feel like a consequence of an evolving world.

## 16.3 Uncertain Threat Information

The game should provide approximate information about enemy discovery or potential attacks without revealing exact calculations.

I want players to understand that danger is increasing while still experiencing uncertainty.

---

# 17. SETTLEMENT CONSTRUCTION AND DEVELOPMENT

Settlement management is intended to become a major part of the game.

## 17.1 Rebuilding and Expansion

I want the player to begin with limited resources and infrastructure.

Over time, the settlement should be capable of developing into something much larger.

The eventual direction depends on how I choose to play.

A heroic character might establish a prosperous refuge.

A villainous character might develop a heavily fortified military settlement controlled through fear.

## 17.2 Construction

I want a reasonably advanced construction system.

Buildings should have actual purposes rather than existing purely as decorations.

Potential building categories include:

* Housing.
* Resource production.
* Storage.
* Crafting.
* Military recruitment.
* Defensive structures.
* Administrative buildings.
* Specialized class or faction infrastructure.

Not every building needs to be available immediately.

Construction should depend on resources, progression, and relevant conditions.

## 17.3 Population Management

I specifically want population mechanics included.

A settlement should not function as an abstract collection of buildings.

Its inhabitants should matter.

Important settlement attributes should include:

* Population.
* Loyalty.
* Morale.
* Productivity.
* Freedom.
* Discipline.
* Available resources.
* Military strength.

These attributes should interact with one another.

For example, a settlement with high population but inadequate resources could experience serious problems.

Likewise, a settlement with strong military discipline but extremely low loyalty might eventually become unstable.

---

# 18. HEROIC AND VILLAINOUS GOVERNANCE

I want settlement management to change according to how I govern.

## 18.1 Heroic Governance

A heroic ruler might prioritize:

* Protecting civilians.
* Rescuing prisoners.
* Accepting refugees.
* Maintaining freedom.
* Improving living conditions.
* Developing diplomatic relationships.
* Supporting citizens.

These choices could improve loyalty and morale.

However, I specifically want trade-offs.

A particularly lenient ruler might experience reduced productivity or weaker discipline.

I do not want being a hero to automatically provide the best possible outcome in every category.

## 18.2 Villainous Governance

A villainous ruler might instead rely on:

* Fear.
* Oppression.
* Forced labor.
* Military authority.
* Strict discipline.
* Conquest.
* Enslavement.
* Punishment.

These systems might provide certain short-term advantages.

For instance, forced labor could increase production under some circumstances.

However, this should create substantial long-term risks.

Examples include:

* Rebellion.
* Sabotage.
* Desertion.
* Civil unrest.
* Low loyalty.
* Diplomatic hostility.

A villainous settlement might become powerful but inherently unstable.

## 18.3 Governance Should Not Be Binary

I want governance to depend on both faction characteristics and my personal morality.

Different civilizations should respond differently to similar policies.

A military-oriented faction might tolerate strict discipline more readily than a civilization that strongly values personal freedom.

Likewise, a ruler's reputation should influence how citizens interpret their decisions.

I want systems that allow meaningful differences between settlements rather than applying identical bonuses and penalties everywhere.

---

# 19. RECRUITMENT AND POPULATION GROWTH

I want different methods of increasing settlement population.

The available methods should depend partly on the player's actions and morality.

Examples include:

**Peaceful recruitment:** Attracting new inhabitants by providing safety and opportunity.

**Rescue:** Freeing captives who may choose to join the player's settlement.

**Diplomatic recruitment:** Establishing relationships that encourage migration.

**Conquest:** Taking control of enemy populations through military expansion.

**Enslavement:** Forcing captured individuals into labor or military service.

These approaches should produce different consequences.

People who voluntarily join a settlement should not necessarily behave the same way as people who were forcibly captured.

I want recruitment methods to affect loyalty, stability, morale, productivity, and reputation.

---

# 20. ARMIES AND MILITARY STRATEGY

## 20.1 Raising Armies

Eventually, I want the player to be capable of creating armies.

This should be a long-term progression objective.

The player should need sufficient resources, population, and infrastructure to support military expansion.

## 20.2 Automated Combat

I do not want every soldier controlled individually.

Instead, armies should follow commands and fight automatically.

The player should focus on broader decisions.

Examples include:

* Choosing military objectives.
* Defending territory.
* Attacking enemy positions.
* Organizing military forces.
* Deciding when to engage or retreat.

The details of formations, unit categories, morale, supply, and battle resolution still need further development.

## 20.3 Integration with Personal Gameplay

Army management should not replace the action RPG portion of the game.

I want both to coexist.

My protagonist should remain an important individual character even after becoming a military leader.

The game should gradually expand in scale without completely abandoning the mechanics introduced at the beginning.

---

# 21. STORY PROGRESSION AND WORLD REACTIVITY

I want the story to progress through escalating arcs.

## Early Game: Survival

My immediate concerns involve surviving, exploring, collecting resources, and understanding what happened to my village.

The protagonist should have relatively little influence over the wider world.

## Early-to-Mid Game: Establishment

I begin strengthening my character and developing relationships.

The settlement becomes more capable.

I encounter rival factions, discover additional regions, and begin making increasingly consequential decisions.

## Mid Game: Political and Military Influence

My actions begin affecting regional conflicts.

I may develop alliances, establish military forces, and influence territorial control.

My moral alignment and reputation should become increasingly important.

## Late Game: Dominion

The player becomes capable of influencing major civilizations.

The final direction depends on previous choices.

I could become a protector, ruler, conqueror, destroyer, or something less easily categorized.

The precise story missions, events, and endings still need to be developed.

I want the narrative to accommodate different playstyles without requiring a completely separate campaign for every possible character.

I want six primary difficulty presets.

They should affect more than enemy health and damage.

Difficulty should influence the overall world.

* Enemy discovery speed.

# 23. ART STYLE AND PRESENTATION

I want Ashes of Dominion to use pixel-art graphics.

However, I do not want the game to feel restricted to a completely flat, simplistic presentation.

## 23.1 2.5D Presentation

My preferred direction is a hybrid 2.5D visual style.

This should combine the readability and character of pixel art with environments that have a sense of depth.

During exploration, the camera should use a three-quarter, top-down perspective.

## 23.2 Visual Variety

Different regions should have distinctive appearances.

For example:

* Human kingdoms could emphasize medieval stone and timber architecture.
* Elven regions could emphasize forests and magical structures.
* Dwarven civilizations could feature mountain fortresses and industrial environments.
* Demonic territories could use volcanic and corrupted landscapes.
* Ancient technological regions could contain unusual mechanical ruins.

I want players to recognize major regions through their visual identity.

## 23.3 Practical Requirements

The art direction should also remain practical for development.

Because I am working on this project through Claude, I want the game structured so environments, animations, and content can be expanded gradually.

I do not want the prototype to require every final art asset before gameplay systems can be tested.

# 24. CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP

I want the fundamental gameplay loop to connect exploration, fighting, progression, and settlement development.

A typical expedition might follow this structure:

**1. Preparation**

I begin at my settlement, prepare my equipment, assess available resources, and decide where I want to travel.

**2. Route Planning**

I examine the world map, select a destination, and evaluate available routes.

Because information can be incomplete, I need to decide how much risk I am willing to accept.

**3. Exploration**

I travel into the world and uncover previously unknown areas.

I may discover resources, encounters, ruins, NPCs, or dangerous terrain.

**4. Combat and Interaction**

I fight enemies, interact with characters, make decisions, and potentially discover valuable items or information.

**5. Return**

I return to the settlement, either normally or through the death-return mechanic if I am defeated.

**6. Development**

I use acquired resources and knowledge to improve my character, equipment, or settlement.

**7. World Progression**

Faction relationships, political conflicts, enemy awareness, and other world systems continue evolving.

The next expedition should therefore take place in a world that may have changed because of previous events.

I want this cycle to remain relevant throughout the game, even as additional systems become available.

# 29. IMPORTANT DESIGN PRINCIPLES

There are several principles I want to preserve throughout development.

### Player Freedom

The player should have meaningful control over their character's progression and moral direction.

I do not want a predetermined heroic narrative.

### Consequences

Actions should have consequences that persist.

Destroying settlements, betraying allies, rescuing prisoners, and conquering territory should influence the world.

### Replayability

Different characters, racial relationships, procedural routes, difficulty settings, and moral choices should make repeated playthroughs meaningfully different.

### Progression

The player should begin relatively weak and gradually become more powerful.

Major accomplishments should feel earned.

### World Reactivity

Civilizations and factions should behave like independent participants in the world.

They should not exist solely to wait for the player to interact with them.

### Strategic Depth

Decisions should involve trade-offs.

There should not always be one mathematically superior answer.

For example, increasing productivity through oppressive governance might create serious political instability.

### Risk and Reward

Exploration should involve uncertainty.

Dangerous routes, rare rewards, companion mortality, and imperfect information should encourage preparation.

### Mechanical Consistency

The different systems should support each other.

Combat, exploration, settlements, morality, factions, and progression should not feel like unrelated minigames.

### Technical Expandability

I want a strong underlying architecture that allows the project to evolve over time.

I do not want early shortcuts to make future development unnecessarily difficult.

# 30. MY LONG-TERM END GOAL

Ultimately, I want Ashes of Dominion to become a large, interconnected fantasy RPG in which the player can influence the world on multiple levels.

At the beginning, I am simply a survivor.

I explore dangerous territory, acquire equipment, learn abilities, and attempt to establish a foothold in a hostile environment.

As I become stronger, the game gradually expands.

I gain access to more advanced character progression.

I develop relationships with NPCs and factions.

I recruit companions.

I improve my settlement.

I become involved in political conflicts.

Eventually, I may establish military forces, conquer territory, and influence the fate of entire civilizations.

Throughout this progression, the world should continue responding to my decisions.

**I want the player to feel that they are gradually becoming a significant force within a living world rather than simply completing a fixed sequence of missions.**

The same character might become a beloved protector in one playthrough and a feared tyrant in another.

Different starting races should experience different challenges.

Different moral decisions should lead to different opportunities and consequences.

Procedural geography should make exploration less predictable while preserving important locations and narrative continuity.

The settlement should reflect my decisions as a ruler.

My companions should feel important because their survival is not guaranteed.

The world's civilizations should continue pursuing their own objectives, regardless of whether I interfere.

And most importantly, the game should remain enjoyable during the early stages, before any of these larger systems become available.

I want the experience of exploring, surviving, fighting, and slowly developing my character to be satisfying on its own.

The larger strategic systems should expand that experience rather than replace it.
